## [TouchBridge: Tactile-Conditioned Human-to-Robot Transfer for Dexterous Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40349v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40349v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 10/10
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** How can contact-rich human demonstrations be transformed into robot-domain training sequences efficiently and faithfully without task-specific robot demonstrations, object meshes, or simulation refinement?

**Claimed contribution:** The authors introduce TouchBridge, a hardware-software co-designed framework that uses TouchBridge-Cap for wearable human demonstration capture and Tac-Retarget for tactile-conditioned retargeting, enabling the transformation of human demonstrations into robot-domain data 7.0 times faster while achieving real-robot policy success rates close to those trained on robot teleoperation.

**Method:** TouchBridge employs an aligned hardware interface with wrist cameras and fingertip tactile sensors, coupled with Tac-Retarget, which combines fingertip pose alignment, contact-patch alignment, and a tactile-activated contact graph, alongside visual synthesis and camera-anchored action construction.

**Datasets/evaluation:** Four contact-rich real-world tasks (Flip Book, Screw Bulb, Apply Sauce, and Grasp Egg Roll), utilizing 60 human demonstrations and 60 teleoperated robot demonstrations per task.

**Baselines:** DexPilot, UniDex, Diffusion Policy, and various ablated variants of Tac-Retarget (w/o contact graph, w/o contact alignment, and kinematic only).

**Reported findings:** Policies fine-tuned on TouchBridge-converted human demonstrations achieve a 72.5% average success rate (compared to 77.5% using teleoperated data), outperforming DexPilot and UniDex by 25.0 and 42.5 percentage points respectively, while reducing contact error to 4.667 mm.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** The current study uses a modest demonstration set, and operators must minimize head motion to reduce frame-to-frame viewpoint changes during capture.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The paper provides implementation details including sensor-to-fingertip mounting transforms, tactile preprocessing thresholds, mathematical formulations for the objectives, and a comprehensive hyperparameter table in the appendix.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Extending the TouchBridge framework to larger and more diverse human datasets remains an important direction for future work.

## [SwapBot: Tool-Centric Robot Manipulation via Interchangeable End-Effectors and Tactile Sensing](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40646v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40646v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 9/9
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** Can a modular, tool-centric end-effector system with integrated tactile sensing and tool-matched teleoperation improve robot performance and imitation learning success in contact-rich, long-horizon manipulation tasks?

**Claimed contribution:** The authors introduce SwapBot, a modular, low-cost tool-centric end-effector system that features rapid autonomous tool swapping, custom piezoresistive tactile sensing, an intuitive teleoperation interface for demonstration collection, and tactile-augmented hierarchical imitation learning policies for long-horizon tasks.

**Method:** SwapBot combines a robot-side electromagnetic coupling hub with low-cost 3D-printed tool adapters supporting passive and articulated tools. It uses a matrix-layout piezoresistive tactile sensor (Velostat sandwiched between conductive fabric) and a hierarchical policy framework consisting of a ResNet-based high-level tool selector and U-net diffusion-based low-level control policies conditioned on visual, proprioceptive, and tactile inputs.

**Datasets/evaluation:** The evaluation utilizes custom demonstration datasets collected via human teleoperation (100 demonstrations per stage for tasks like brushing, transporting, and cutting pan-fried tofu) and evaluated over multi-trial robot execution rollouts and human user studies with 12 participants.

**Baselines:** Baselines include a general VR-style teleoperation tool, SwapBot without tactile feedback, policies trained without tactile sensing (RGB-only), top-down RGB with tactile, and egocentric RGB with tactile.

**Reported findings:** SwapBot reduces task completion time and operator workload in user studies. For autonomous execution, policies trained with tactile sensing achieve higher success rates, increasing tofu-cooking success from 35% (RGB-only) to 60%, and absolute policy success rates by 15–25 percentage points across contact-rich skills.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** Tactile sensors remain low resolution and prone to wear, sensor placement is fixed by human intuition, and policy learning depends on predefined task segmentation.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The project website is provided (https://personalrobotics.github.io/SwapBot), hardware components and fabrication details are described, and the authors state they will open-source the system.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work will explore more durable and higher-resolution sensing, data-driven strategies for sensor placement, and autonomous policy sequencing without manual subgoal definitions.
