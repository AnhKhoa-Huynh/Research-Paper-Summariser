## [ForceLDM: A Force-aware Latent Dynamics Model for Contact-Rich Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03294v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03294v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 23/23
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** How to perform contact-rich robotic manipulation requiring fine-grained control and complex physical interactions when vision-only policies struggle to infer contact states and forces?

**Claimed contribution:** The paper proposes ForceLDM, a force-aware latent dynamics model that distills future force/torque and optical-flow dynamics from a privileged teacher into a student policy, enabling it to model future contact and motion dynamics to guide action generation using only current observations at inference time.

**Method:** ForceLDM combines a vision-language-action (VLA) backbone, a future dynamics encoder (using future 6D force/torque measurements and dense optical flow processed via a pretrained image VAE and Q-Former), an online future-dynamics distillation framework aligning intermediate representations via cosine similarity with stop-gradient, curriculum-based progressive noise injection, and flow matching for action generation.

**Datasets/evaluation:** Five real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks using an xArm 7 manipulator (collecting 80-100 successful demonstration trajectories per task, totaling 420 demonstrations and 332,237 frames across USB Insertion, Gear Assembly, Box Flipping, Board Wiping, and VGA Plugging) and a supplementary AgileX Piper-X manipulator setup.

**Baselines:** The baselines compared against include pi_0 w/o force, pi_0 w/ force, TA-VLA, ForceVLA, and Seer w/ force.

**Reported findings:** ForceLDM achieves the highest average success rate of 87.1% on in-distribution tasks (outperforming Seer w/ force at 72.2%) and 68.9% average success rate on out-of-distribution generalization settings (outperforming Seer w/ force at 48.9%).

**Limitations/threats to validity:** The experiments use small-scale datasets, optical-flow estimation errors caused by occlusion or motion blur may affect the learned dynamics representations, and 2D optical flow lacks explicit depth information.

**Reproducibility checklist:** Project page and code are available at https://tpy001.github.io/ForceLDM/. Detailed model architecture, hyperparameters, and dataset collection procedures are provided in the appendices.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work will explore scaling up training data to improve performance and combining 3D scene flow with force feedback to better model future interaction dynamics.

## [Pixel-UMI: Learning Environment-Aware Whole-Body Manipulation from Sensorized Human Demonstrations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03647v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03647v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 15/15
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** How can environment-aware whole-body manipulation policies be effectively learned from sensorized human demonstrations while addressing human-to-robot embodiment gaps and contact limitations?

**Claimed contribution:** The paper presents Pixel-UMI, a pipeline introducing Environment-Aware Retargeting (EAR) for collision-aware whole-body joint and base supervision, Retargeting-Error-Aware Masking (REAM) to reduce reliance on mismatched camera views, and on-policy DAgger correction for contact-rich tasks.

**Method:** The method uses offline metric 3D scene reconstruction from demonstrations, separates static and dynamic geometry, and applies geometric fabrics for collision-aware retargeting. It then calibrates joint tracking delays for action assignment, uses retargeting errors for error-aware camera masking during policy training, and gathers DAgger corrections.

**Datasets/evaluation:** The evaluation utilizes five tasks: Arrange Mouse (100 demonstrations), Pack Tennis Canister (178 demonstrations), Retrieve Drink (219 demonstrations), Fold Box (393 demonstrations), and Hang Jacket (261 demonstrations).

**Baselines:** Baselines and ablation conditions include retargeting without environment awareness, EEF-Rel Base and Joint Rel action representations, next-state action labels, wrist-only observations, relaxed head tracking, and policies trained without REAM or without human demonstrations.

**Reported findings:** Across five tasks, Pixel-UMI achieves an 87.0% average task progress. EAR increases average progress by 64.41 pp on three tasks, REAM improves it by 14.05 pp, action calibration adds 24.05 pp, and two DAgger rounds increase progress by 72.95 pp on contact-rich tasks.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** Delay-calibrated action assignment cannot recover contact-dependent forces, single-camera scene reconstructions leave occluded surfaces incomplete, and the framework does not explicitly capture intentional contacts involving body parts other than the head and grippers.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The paper provides architectural details, hyperparameters (such as ρ = 0.8 and pmax = 0.9 for REAM), training schedules (150,000 optimizer updates), and a public project website at https://pixel-robot.com/pixel-umi.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work could incorporate force/torque or tactile sensing to capture interaction wrenches, use multi-view sensors to improve scene reconstruction, and develop retargeting objectives that explicitly model non-gripper body contacts.
