## [Body Schema Policy: Body as the Vision-Action Interface for Efficient Robot Loco-Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22713v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22713v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 11/11
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** Can a visuomotor policy learn whole-body skills as efficiently as humans do?

**Claimed contribution:** We introduce Body Schema Policy (BSP), an imitation learning policy that builds a human-inspired latent body schema into the vision-action interface for efficient robot loco-manipulation.

**Method:** BSP models the robot embodiment as a kinematic tree where a Neural Screw Operator propagates link features, body-queried vision retrieves visual evidence independently for each body part, and body-aware action denoising conditions a shared temporal decoder.

**Datasets/evaluation:** Nine simulated Unitree G1 tasks in SIMPLE, real-world Unitree G1 tasks, and three ManiSkill-HAB SetTable subtasks on a Fetch mobile manipulator.

**Baselines:** Diffusion Policy, Rodrigues Network, Patch Policy, pi0.5, GR00T N1.6, Psi_0, and DreamZero.

**Reported findings:** On simulated G1 tasks, the 35M-parameter BSP matches the success rate of the 2.63B-parameter Psi_0 foundation model with 75x fewer parameters and nearly doubles Diffusion Policy's success rate.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** Due to limited compute, the authors could not scale training and evaluation on SIMPLE to more seeds, and the latent body schema is less effective when whole-body controllers are commanded in a latent space rather than joint space.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The paper provides model parameters, network designs, training recipes, dataset details, and a public project website.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work includes cross-embodiment policies conditioning on the latent body schema, integration into WAMs and VLAs, and building compact libraries of atomic skills for agentic systems.
