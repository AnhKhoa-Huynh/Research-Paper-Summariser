## [WiTac: Wrist-Informed Tactile Representations for Contact-Rich Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21057v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21057v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 17/17
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** Does WiTac improve real-world contact-rich manipulation over base policies and tactile encoders without wrist fusion, how does wrist conditioning affect contact representations and robustness, and how do pretraining and visual supervision contribute to performance?

**Claimed contribution:** We propose WiTac, a pretrained contact encoder that fuses tactile histories, hand kinematics, and wrist observations into finger-level contact tokens before action generation, and demonstrate improved success rates and better representation properties across four contact-rich tasks.

**Method:** WiTac summarizes ten-finger tactile histories, robot state, and wrist-relative fingertip kinematics, then queries wrist patch features through cross-attention using two Transformer blocks. The encoder is pretrained via self-supervised objectives (contact reconstruction, masked visual recovery, and wrench response prediction) on SharpaDex v1.0 and freezes at policy fine-tuning to condition VLA action policies via gated cross-attention.

**Datasets/evaluation:** SharpaDex v1.0 (nearly 300 hours of bimanual dexterous manipulation data across 59 tasks, with 200 teleoperated demonstrations per evaluation task).

**Baselines:** Vision-only base policies (GR00T N1.7 and pi0.5), WiTac-NW (wrist-free variant), VT-MUSE joint representation, FTP-1, and T-Rex.

**Reported findings:** Across four real-world contact-rich tasks, WiTac improves average success over vision-only policies by +28.8% (GR00T N1.7) and +15.0% (pi0.5), and outperforms WiTac-NW by 18.8% and 7.5%, respectively.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** WiTac uses feature-level cross-attention without depth-based alignment of vision and touch in a shared 3D space, combines tactile histories with only current wrist images without explicitly modeling their joint temporal evolution, and is evaluated on only four tasks on a single robotic platform.

**Reproducibility checklist:** Yes, implementation details, pretraining objectives, temporal offsets, objective weights, and downstream policy fine-tuning hyperparameters are provided in Appendix A, and the project page is publicly available.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work could explore depth-based alignment of vision and touch in a shared 3D space, explicitly modeling joint temporal evolution between tactile histories and wrist images, and scaling evaluations to diverse hand morphologies, tactile sensors, and camera configurations.

## [Cooperative Mobile Manipulation using Learned Dynamical Systems](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21198v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21198v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 15/15
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** How to enable multiple mobile manipulators to coordinate learned single-agent dynamical systems behaviors in shared workspaces while ensuring whole-board collision avoidance and task constraints?

**Claimed contribution:** The paper proposes CALDS (Cooperative Adaptation of Learned Dynamical Systems), a framework consisting of Cooperative Policy Selection and Temporal Adaptation to coordinate learned single-agent behaviors for mobile manipulators while ensuring whole-body collision avoidance and task constraints.

**Method:** CALDS utilizes two main centralized stages: Cooperative Policy Selection, which identifies compatible combinations of single-agent learned dynamical systems policies by ranking them, and Temporal Adaptation, which adjusts velocity scaling factors to achieve collision-free collaboration while preserving the spatial structure and convergence properties of the original policies using gradient-based optimization in JAX.

**Datasets/evaluation:** The framework is evaluated in three simulated collaborative scenarios: assistive dressing, restaurant cleaning and serving, and drawer opening and object placement.

**Baselines:** Collaborative policy adaptation via ONCol-DMPs, whole-body collision avoidance via control barrier functions combined with nominal single-agent policies (CBF-CDSM and CBF-PUMA), and an ablation using nominal learned DSs.

**Reported findings:** CALDS-CDSM and CALDS-PUMA achieve success rates between 91% and 100% across the three scenarios, outperforming baselines and nominal ablations while maintaining small path deviations.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** Not reported

**Reproducibility checklist:** The paper provides an appendix with experimental details, lists hyperparameters, and mentions that code will be open-sourced upon publication alongside an accompanying video.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Integrating the framework into a single neural network conditioned on discrete markers representing different behavioral modes, and extending it to human-robot collaboration.
