## [Learning Heterogeneous Tactile Representation for General Dexterous Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12806v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12806v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 22/27
**Coverage:** partial/truncated text

**Research question:** How to learn a heterogeneous tactile representation for general dexterous manipulation from multi-source tactile data and apply it effectively to downstream robot policies?

**Claimed contribution:** Developing LingBot-Touch, a tactile foundation model that combines a canonical fingertip space, modality-specific tokenizers, and a shared Haptic Transformer trained with a latent-prediction objective (Tactile-JEPA) on a 5,000-hour multi-source tactile corpus, successfully transferring to unseen sensors and improving real-world and simulated robot manipulation policies.

**Method:** LingBot-Touch maps sensor taxels into a canonical spherical fingertip space using continuous 3D coordinates and finger identities, tokenizes signals via modality-specific MLPs, and processes them through a shared Haptic Transformer with 5D rotary positional embeddings using a joint-embedding predictive architecture (Tactile-JEPA).

**Datasets/evaluation:** A multi-source tactile dataset comprising 4,983 hours of recordings, 464,431 episodes, and 1.33 billion frames across piezoresistive, magnetic, and visuotactile sensing modalities covering 24 task categories.

**Baselines:** FTP-1 tactile encoder with sensor-specific adapters, standard VAE and MAE signal reconstruction models, and baseline robot policies without tactile input or with raw tactile signals.

**Reported findings:** Frozen-encoder probing shows LingBot-Touch outperforms FTP-1 on contact physics recovery and tactile-to-video retrieval (e.g., 72.63% top-5 recall on Sharpa Wave). In real-world multi-policy experiments, adding LingBot-Touch tokens raises success rates across 26 tasks by 14 to 26 percentage points, significantly outperforming raw tactile inputs.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** Real-world and simulation experiments need evaluation on a wider variety of hand embodiments, capacitive sensors are currently absent from pretraining, and layout coverage focuses primarily on fingertips rather than palms or entire fingers.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The inference code, sample tactile recordings, and model checkpoints for Tiny and Small variants pretrained on approximately 5,000 hours of tactile data will be released.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Extending layout coverage from fingertips to whole-hand embodiments, incorporating capacitive sensors into the pretraining corpus, and exploring further applications in diverse embodied intelligence tasks.

## [Reins: Frontier Multimodal Models Driving Real Robot Arms on Long-Horizon Tasks, Zero-Shot](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16632v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16632v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 9/9
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** Can off-the-shelf frontier multimodal models drive real dual-arm robot hardware on contact-rich, long-horizon manipulation tasks zero-shot using a harness with 3D perception tools and a continuous narrator?

**Claimed contribution:** The paper presents Reins, a harness that enables off-the-shelf frontier multimodal models to operate a dual-arm robot using guarded action chains, 3D perception tools, and a continuous narrator instance that tracks scene changes.

**Method:** Reins uses an actor model that executes tool calls and guarded chains (stopping when conditions fail or repeats are exhausted) while a second narrator model instance watches the head camera at 1 frame per second to provide a running account of scene changes.

**Datasets/evaluation:** Five real-world robot tasks evaluated over five episodes per condition: a shell game, a drawer opening and filling task, towel folding, shirt folding, and a sweep task.

**Baselines:** GPT-6.1 Sol without the narrator, GPT-6.1 Sol without the harness, GPT-6 Astra, and Claude Opus 5.5.

**Reported findings:** With natural-language prompts and no task-specific code, GPT-6.1 Sol succeeded in 88% of real episodes across the five tasks, whereas Sol without the harness failed every shell game and took 8 times as long.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** Task knowledge enters solely through natural-language prompts, contact is sensed only from joint current and gripper width, only one hardware platform and five episodes per condition are tested, and slow narrators report fast changes late.

**Reproducibility checklist:** Code will be released upon publication. The paper details model tiers, tool definitions, caching strategies, and context management parameters.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Turning successful logged episodes into reusable chains and checks rehearsed in simulation twins so the model finishes tasks faster from shorter prompts.
