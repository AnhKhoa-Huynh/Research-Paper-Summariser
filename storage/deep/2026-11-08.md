## [H^2MARL: Hierarchical Heterogeneous Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Robot Control](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06523v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06523v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 8/8
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** How to achieve coordinated whole-body control for humanoid robots by addressing high-dimensional coupled dynamics and physical heterogeneity across joint degrees of freedom?

**Claimed contribution:** The paper proposes H2MARL, featuring a JDoF-level physical-heterogeneity-aware policy factorization, a hierarchical policy-executor coupling mechanism for closed-loop co-optimization, and task-specific auxiliary rewards.

**Method:** H2MARL formulates whole-body control as a cooperative heterogeneous multi-agent problem at the JDoF level using physical descriptors, combines a high-level pose increment planner with a low-level temporal joint executor (HST) using historical proprioception, and co-optimizes them using shared closed-loop interaction trajectories.

**Datasets/evaluation:** MuJoCo and Isaac Gym benchmarks (specifically using the Humanoid-v2 walking task).

**Baselines:** HAPPO, MAPPO, HATD3, and single-agent, body part-level, and limb-level formulation variants.

**Reported findings:** H2MARL outperforms baselines in metrics such as Foot Slip, Torso Stability, and Gait Symmetry (e.g., improving FS, TS, and GS over MAPPO by 7.3%, 35.0%, and 14.8%, respectively), while achieving a total inference time of 9.259 ms.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** JDoF-level heterogeneous formulation introduces more frequent joint-level adjustments, which slightly reduces motion smoothness compared to MAPPO.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The paper specifies network architectures, hyperparameters such as scaling factors and reward coefficients (e.g., alpha=0.001, Kp=100, Kd=5), training steps (10 million), and hardware used (RTX 4090 GPU and Intel Xeon Gold 6530 CPU), but does not provide open-source code repositories.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Investigate scaling H2MARL to more complex loco-manipulation tasks and test the framework on physical hardware platforms.

## [Which Physical Tactile Cues Matter for Contact-Rich Imitation Learning? A Controlled Real-Robot Study](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06673v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06673v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 6/6
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** Which physical tactile cues, representations, encoders, and visuo-tactile fusion strategies matter most for contact-rich imitation learning?

**Claimed contribution:** The paper introduces an open, LeRobot-compatible visuo-tactile platform and standardized Diffusion Policy pipeline, conducts a controlled comparative study of physical tactile cues (appearance, geometry, and tangential deformation), and evaluates generic versus pretrained tactile encoders alongside visuo-tactile fusion strategies across 720 real-robot trials.

**Method:** A LeRobot platform equipped with a vision-based tactile sensor derives tactile RGB, normal/depth maps, and marker-flow fields from the same tactile stream, which are evaluated under a shared Diffusion Policy backbone across three manipulation tasks.

**Datasets/evaluation:** Real-robot demonstrations collected using a LeRobot-compatible visuo-tactile parallel-jaw gripper, evaluated across 30 trials per configuration (720 total trials).

**Baselines:** Vision-only policies using dual-camera vision (global and wrist cameras) without tactile input.

**Reported findings:** Incorporating tactile perception increased aggregate success rates from 57.8% to 78.9%, with marker flow achieving the highest individual cue success rate at 74.4% and full tactile achieving 78.9%. Cross-attention fusion further improved ResNet-18 performance from 78.9% to 85.6% and Sparsh-DINO from 83.3% to 87.8%.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** The study is limited by the absence of direct force measurements, differences in frontend architectures across tactile cues, and evaluation restricted to a single tactile sensor, a parallel-jaw gripper, and three tabletop tasks.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The authors open-source their LeRobot-compatible visuo-tactile testbed and provide complete experimental parameters, task setups, and trial counts over 720 real-robot executions.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work will investigate the physical roles of tactile representations and their generalization across diverse sensors, novel objects, and various robot embodiments.
