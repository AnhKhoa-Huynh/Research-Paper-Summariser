## [FORGE: Force-Aware Multi-Granularity Control for Efficient Humanoid Loco-Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17142v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17142v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 8/8
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** How can humanoid robots achieve efficient, force-aware loco-manipulation combining velocity navigation, planar foot placement, end-effector tracking, and contact adaptation without relying on full-body human motion references or dedicated wrist force/torque sensors?

**Claimed contribution:** The paper proposes FORGE, a force-aware multi-granularity control system spanning base velocity navigation, planar foot placement, wrist-pose tracking, and optional force-conditioning inputs, trained without full-body human motion references and augmented with a physics-informed hybrid force estimator and asymmetric residual actions.

**Method:** FORGE utilizes a unified coarse-to-fine command interface paired with a Physics-Informed Hybrid Force Estimator that combines an arm momentum observer with a learned correction using simulator contact-force supervision. It employs granularity-conditioned observation and reward masking alongside an asymmetric residual whole-body control policy anchored on a frozen locomotion prior.

**Datasets/evaluation:** The evaluation uses simulated tasks (such as box contact and transport) and real-world hardware experiments on a G1 humanoid, including 20 hardware trials per method for control comparisons, staged load trials for force estimation, and 80 demonstrations per payload condition (320 total across empty, 0.5 kg, 1 kg, and 2 kg payloads) for upstream Diffusion Policy execution.

**Baselines:** HOMIE, TWIST2, SONIC, observer-only and learned-only (UniFP-style) force estimators, and end-to-end 29-DoF policies without a locomotion prior or masking.

**Reported findings:** FORGE achieved lower tracking errors and contact forces on hardware compared to baselines (e.g., foot MPE of 5.42 cm, wrist MPE of 4.15 cm, and simulated bilateral mean absolute normal reaction of 8.52 N). In downstream diffusion-policy box-transport tasks across empty, 0.5 kg, 1 kg, and 2 kg payloads, it achieved success rates of 100%, 100%, 90%, and 90%, respectively.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** Not reported.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The paper provides architectural details, hyperparameter values, reward formulations, and open-source project website information (bb0928.github.io/forge).

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work could explore scaling the multi-granularity command interface to more complex unmodeled contact environments, integrating more diverse visuo-motor task planners, and evaluating sensorless force estimation under broader dynamic and highly non-quasi-static conditions.

## [Grana: Compositional Reward Modeling for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24926v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24926v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 23/23
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** How to design a generalizable robot reward model that accurately tracks progress in long-horizon manipulation while leveraging short-task pretraining and open-vocabulary subtask descriptions?

**Claimed contribution:** We introduce Grana, a vision-language robot reward model that learns to identify unfinished subtasks and estimate their continuous progress using open-vocabulary subtask descriptions, along with a structured annotation protocol and demonstrations of improved downstream policy learning and long-horizon adaptation.

**Method:** Grana shares a pretrained vision-language model (Qwen3.5-4B) with LoRA adapters, a temporal transformer, and a progress head to select the unfinished subtask and predict its local progress, which is combined with credit for preceding completed subtasks to estimate whole-task progress.

**Datasets/evaluation:** The training corpus includes 200 diverse mostly short-horizon manipulation tasks, 50 held-out tasks for zero-shot evaluation, five long-horizon tasks (circuit assembly, firetruck assembly, airplane assembly, red car assembly, and Pupgo car assembly), and annotation studies using 100 trajectories.

**Baselines:** SARM, SARM2, RoboMeter (official and finetuned), GPT-6 Astra, and direct whole-task predictors.

**Reported findings:** Grana achieves 20.7% lower progress RMSE than the strongest evaluated baseline on 50 held-out tasks, attains the lowest RMSE across five long-horizon tasks when adapted with 15 labeled trajectories, and yields the highest observed success rates on held-out tasks in offline reinforcement learning (65.0% on star insertion and 66.7% on tube transfer).

**Limitations/threats to validity:** Not reported

**Reproducibility checklist:** Code and models are stated to be available via the project website https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/grana/, with training and model hyperparameters detailed in the appendices.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Extending compositional reward modeling to even longer horizons, fully autonomous zero-shot deployment in unstructured open-world environments, and integrating richer feedback signals for active failure recovery.
