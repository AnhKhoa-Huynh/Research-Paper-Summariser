## [Online Learning of Heterogeneous Locomotion Dynamics under Reinforcement Learning Policies for Precise Loco-Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41892v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41892v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 17/18
**Coverage:** partial/truncated text

**Research question:** How can the heterogeneity of closed-loop dynamics under a frozen RL locomotion policy be accounted for to achieve precise loco-manipulation?

**Claimed contribution:** A contaminated Gaussian model is proposed to approximate latent-conditioned heterogeneous responses, combined with a posterior-weighted RFF online learner and a residual-augmented MPC controller to achieve precise loco-manipulation without retraining the policy.

**Method:** The method uses a contaminated Gaussian residual model with reference covariance and inflated covariance, an RFF-based Gaussian process online learner weighted by posterior unlearnability probabilities, and a residual-augmented MPC that regulates feedforward compensation using epistemic uncertainty.

**Datasets/evaluation:** Simulation and hardware experiments using an M1 quadruped robot equipped with Kinova Gen2 and AgileX Piper manipulators in MuJoCo and physical environments.

**Baselines:** Nominal MPC and SSI-MPC (online residual learning with a homogeneous RFF residual model and gradient descent updates).

**Reported findings:** Simulation and hardware results demonstrate consistent end-effector tracking across diverse tasks with errors of approximately 0.01 m, outperforming Nominal MPC and SSI-MPC.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** The contaminated Gaussian model provides only an approximation of heterogeneity and does not explicitly address how to maintain tracking accuracy under nonreference latent conditions.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The paper provides an explicit finite-sample error bound for online learning, a rollout prediction error bound, algorithmic descriptions (Algorithm 1), and parameter settings.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work will explore multiple Gaussian processes to learn the mapping from observable states to latent conditions and develop closed-loop stability guarantees for the interaction between online learning, MPC, and the frozen policy.

## [RoboAtom: Large-scale Atomic-Action Supervision for Robot Learning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40207v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40207v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 23/31
**Coverage:** partial/truncated text

**Research question:** How to construct unified, time-aligned atomic-action annotations across heterogeneous robot datasets at scale, and how to evaluate their utility for automatic annotation and hierarchical robot control?

**Claimed contribution:** The authors introduce RoboAtom-Data, a dataset containing 1.45 million robot trajectories with unified time-aligned atomic-action annotations; RoboAtom-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating skill recognition and temporal localization; RoboAtom-Anno, a high-performing annotation model; and a scalable annotation propagation method named PhysExtend.

**Method:** The method uses human seed annotation followed by PhysExtend, a same-task propagation method that localizes boundaries using skill-specific physical cues, dynamic programming, and monotonic projection. For supervision and control, the authors train RoboAtom-Anno using supervised fine-tuning and GRPO reinforcement learning, and implement a dual-system robot control architecture where System 2 plans atomic actions and System 1 executes them via pi0.5.

**Datasets/evaluation:** RoboAtom-Data (16 sources, 1.45 million trajectories, 6.88 million segments, 16,882.1 hours), RoboAtom-Bench (500 trajectories, 4,018 ground-truth segments), WGO-Bench Robot, LIBERO-Long, LIBERO-Plus, and RoboTwin 2.0.

**Baselines:** GPT-4o, GPT-5.6-sol, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen3.8-27B, Qwen3.5-9B, Cortex, and standard task-level pi0.5 execution.

**Reported findings:** RoboAtom-Anno-9B achieves a Skill F1@0.7 of 30.1 on RoboAtom-Bench (outperforming GPT-5.6-sol's 21.4) and a temporal F1@0.7 of 48.3 on WGO-Bench Robot, while reducing inference costs. In robot control, atomic-action supervision paired with hierarchical execution improves task success to 98.20% on LIBERO-Long, 88.18% overall on LIBERO-Plus, and 66.17% on RoboTwin.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** The evaluation is limited in training scope and data coverage, lacking large-scale policy pretraining integration and egocentric human videos whose brief actions complicate boundary localization.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The authors state they will release the data, benchmark, model, and construction pipeline.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Extending the annotation pipeline and models to handle large-scale egocentric human videos and integrating atomic-action supervision directly into large-scale policy pretraining.
