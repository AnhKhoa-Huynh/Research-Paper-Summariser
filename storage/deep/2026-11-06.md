## [LumiTac: Compact, High-Rate Six-Axis Optical Tactile Sensing for Human-Robot Interaction](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05936v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05936v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 9/9
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** How to design a compact, high-rate six-axis optical tactile sensor (LumiTac) for human-robot interaction and robotic manipulation?

**Claimed contribution:** A miniaturized optoelectronic tactile sensor head measuring 12 mm in diameter and 4.75 mm in thickness with a mass below 2 g, an efficient multilayer perceptron reconstruction pipeline achieving high R2 scores and low MAEs, and experimental validation in real-time tracking and robotic interactions.

**Method:** LumiTac uses opposing circuit boards coupled through translucent silicone containing five photoemitters and five RGBW photoreceivers to measure load-induced interlayer motion, processed by a compact multilayer perceptron (MLP) mapping 20 optical channels to three forces and three moments.

**Datasets/evaluation:** An independent test recording excluded from training was used for evaluation, along with continuous experimental recordings involving standard weight loading, rapid tapping, combined loading, and robotic interaction.

**Baselines:** Table I compares LumiTac against representative multiaxis tactile and force/torque sensors including LiVec finger, El-Azizi et al., F-TOUCH, Mao et al., CoinFT, and HexaTouch.

**Reported findings:** Independent testing yielded coefficients of determination (R2) of 0.9657-0.9941, mean absolute errors of 0.054-0.190 N for forces and 0.304-1.419 mN*m for moments, and an average online prediction update rate of 303.57 Hz with a median software processing latency of 0.722 ms.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** Ambient light, optical alignment, and elastomer nonlinearity remain design and calibration considerations.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The paper details component layouts, network architecture parameters (two hidden layers with 96 and 48 neurons, 6,966 parameters), normalization procedures, and processing rates.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work will explore integration into dexterous hands, wearable interfaces, and the feet of legged robots for broader applications in robotic manipulation, human-robot interaction, and foot-ground interaction sensing.

## [TactileLab: Sim-to-Real Dexterous Robot Manipulation with Efficient Shear-Sensitive Tactile Simulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07881v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07881v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 16/18
**Coverage:** partial/truncated text

**Research question:** How to enable efficient sim-to-real dexterous robot manipulation using shear-sensitive tactile simulation and multimodal tactile representations?

**Claimed contribution:** The paper introduces TactileLab, a scalable multimodal tactile simulation framework built on IsaacLab that uses a novel simulated tactile flow modality alongside contact depth and low-dimensional contact information for contact-rich manipulation tasks.

**Method:** TactileLab utilizes rigid-body simulation combined with GPU-accelerated tactile simulation and simulated tactile flow to estimate dense tangential motion without explicit soft-body physics, coupled with a two-stage teacher-student RL framework and PETS-Net for policy learning.

**Datasets/evaluation:** The authors collected paired real and simulated tactile observations using a contact-and-shear protocol across several sensors including GelSight Mini, DIGIT, 9DTact, and TacTip.

**Baselines:** Proprioception-only (HORA-style) and proprioception plus low-dimensional contact information (AnyRotate-style).

**Reported findings:** In simulation and real-world evaluations, policies using contact depth and tactile flow achieved superior performance, completing more rotations in in-hand manipulation and lower tracking errors in object-following compared to baselines.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** The real-to-sim tactile transfer assumes that the elastomer remains in sticking contact with the object surface rather than slipping.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The project webpage is provided at https://yijionglin.github.io/tactilelab/ and the framework will be released as open source, though source code for all components is not fully provided in the text.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Extending the method to account for slip-detection capabilities and scaling to soft-body tactile simulation frameworks for higher-fidelity physics modeling.
