## [NSDL: Neuro-Symbolic Division of Labor for Multi-Robot Task Planning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16671v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16671v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 10/10
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** Which decisions in multi-robot task planning should be learned by a small vision-language model versus computed by a deterministic solver?

**Claimed contribution:** A neuro-symbolic framework (NSDL) where a trained model performs state judgments and writes a plan sketch, while a deterministic solver expands it into an executable joint plan, alongside a new benchmark (LOCUS-Bench) that separates state-judgment and structural-planning difficulties.

**Method:** The trained model takes an instruction, scene image, and robot capabilities, and uses a detector and deterministic solver within an agentic trajectory to iteratively produce a plan sketch and receive structured execution feedback.

**Datasets/evaluation:** The public VIKI-L2 benchmark (1800 ID and 1218 OOD rows) and the newly introduced LOCUS-Bench (6300 train rows, 180 ID test rows, and 380 OOD test rows across three OOD splits).

**Baselines:** End-to-end planners (VIKI-R 7B, Ans-SFT 7B, zero-shot models), formalize-then-plan baselines (prompt-only, full-trained translation), and an NSDL variant without the solver.

**Reported findings:** NSDL-7B achieves 97.0% out-of-distribution accuracy on VIKI-L2, significantly outperforming end-to-end and formalize-then-plan baselines, while nearly eliminating ordering and scheduling errors.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** Remaining failures are primarily caused by model-misjudged states or omitted goal listings rather than structural planning errors.

**Reproducibility checklist:** Code, model weights, and LOCUS-Bench are publicly available at https://github.com/Xincheng-Tan/NSDL.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work will teach the model which goals to list and extend NSDL to physical execution with closed-loop feedback and a larger action model.

## [HALO: Human-Aligned Data Collection for WhoLe-body MObile Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16439v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16439v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 15/15
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** How can whole-body mobile manipulation data collection be made more efficient and aligned with human natural locomotion and perception?

**Claimed contribution:** We introduce HALO, a human-aligned data collection system for whole-body mobile manipulation that aligns the operator perception-action space with the robot while preserving natural locomotion and direct physical feedback.

**Method:** HALO combines bimanual kinesthetic teaching with a low-resistance passive mobile collection platform, supplemented by deployment-aligned video processing (hand segmentation and inpainting) and base motion alignment.

**Datasets/evaluation:** The paper evaluates tasks using custom setups including Push Chair, Disassemble Lego, and Zip-Tie Fastening, along with a six-stage long-horizon mobile manipulation task.

**Baselines:** Mobile ALOHA and VR-based teleoperation interfaces.

**Reported findings:** HALO enables up to 2.9x faster demonstration collection than Mobile ALOHA, improves success rates on tasks like Disassemble Lego by 20 percentage points, and achieves a 73.3% success rate on a six-stage long-horizon mobile manipulation task.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** The close human-robot coupling introduces visual and control distribution shifts between demonstration data and autonomous deployment, requiring extensive post-processing.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The paper describes hyperparameter configurations, network architectures (pi_0.5), training steps (5k), and hardware components (AgileX Tracer AGV 2, industrial measuring wheels), but does not explicitly state code or hardware repository availability.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work could incorporate active perception to adapt the robot viewpoint during manipulation to more closely reflect human visual behavior.
