## [MRBench: Benchmarking Physical Necessity Reasoning in Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32803v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32803v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 18/23
**Coverage:** partial/truncated text

**Research question:** Which physical capabilities does a task truly require, and which robots or objects are necessary to provide them?

**Claimed contribution:** MRBench, a benchmark for physical necessity reasoning, and MRScissors, a framework separating task requirements from measured physical capabilities through constrained search.

**Method:** MRScissors uses a blind model prover to generate a job graph, code tool calls to measure a capability table, and exact search to determine minimal support sets (MSS) and certificates.

**Datasets/evaluation:** MRBench comprises eight tasks spanning concurrency and reach constraints, evaluated under five configurations across 40 versions and 480 scenes in Isaac Sim.

**Baselines:** Oracle-script, Oracle-team, LLM + solver, SMART-LLM-style, RoCo-style, and HMAS-2-style.

**Reported findings:** MRScissors achieves 85.4% and 91.2% team-selection accuracy across two backbones, outperforming the strongest baselines by 10.4 and 9.5 points.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** Passive substitution remains challenging, with MRScissors missing a third of borrow episodes even with the stronger backbone.

**Reproducibility checklist:** Evaluated in Isaac Sim 4.5 with Isaac Lab 2.1 using standardized tool budgets, though full source code availability is not explicitly detailed beyond the provided website.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Improving agent reasoning for passive object substitution and generalized functional replacement in heterogeneous multi-robot manipulation.

## [DeCoNav-Act: Benchmarking Collective Physical Capability in Multi-Robot Mobile Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32683v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32683v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 23/33
**Coverage:** partial/truncated text

**Research question:** How can multi-robot mobile manipulation be benchmarked to evaluate collective physical capability, particularly regarding language-guided tasks, coordination mechanisms, and handoff successes?

**Claimed contribution:** DeCoNav-Act, a benchmark for language-guided multi-robot mobile manipulation in a shared physics simulation with 500 tasks, a two-level evaluation protocol, and an empirical study showing current agent limitations.

**Method:** A simulation benchmark evaluating teams of robots with sensor-grounded adaptations of ASPIRE, RAX, and CoMoMa across parallel workloads and physical dependency coordination mechanisms, featuring an end-to-end evaluation and a handoff-isolated diagnostic.

**Datasets/evaluation:** DeCoNav-Act benchmark featuring 350 homogeneous and 150 heterogeneous tasks across 118 scenes, 9 room types, and 2,631 object instances, with a 100-task test split.

**Baselines:** Sensor-grounded, multi-robot adaptations of ASPIRE and RAX, evaluated with and without scene priors.

**Reported findings:** With GPT-5.6-luna, no method completes a task end-to-end, at most 8.8% of dependencies are reached, and even from pre-handoff states no handoff is completed; with GPT-6.1-sol, CoMoMa establishes the condition in 26.5% of trials, but only 4.1% succeed.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** Not reported

**Reproducibility checklist:** Not reported

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Extending the benchmark with longer horizons, larger teams, and explicit task allocation, alongside developing agents that reason explicitly about the states they leave for teammates.
