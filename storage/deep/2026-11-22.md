## [I See You: Initial Multi-Robot LiDAR Registration with Mutual-Observation Checks](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26276v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26276v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 8/8
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** How can multi-robot LiDAR point cloud registration be made robust for initial pre-mission alignment in infrastructure-free search-and-rescue environments without relying on external aids?

**Claimed contribution:** A centralized, infrastructure-free framework combining global and local point cloud alignment with deterministic physical plausibility checks (bounding-box overlap and mutual observation) to actively reject geometrically impossible poses, along with an open-source implementation and a synchronized multi-robot LiDAR dataset with ground truth.

**Method:** The framework executes pairwise coarse-to-fine registration (using KISS-Matcher and GICP restricted to the yaw angle), applies deterministic bounding-box overlap and mutual-observation checks, builds a maximum-weight spanning tree, recovers failed edges via tree-seeded refinement, and optimizes all verified edges globally using GTSAM.

**Datasets/evaluation:** A custom dataset collected across seven indoor and outdoor scenarios (Lab, Parking, Walkway, Campus, Forest, Meadow, Park) using four heterogeneous UGVs equipped with diverse LiDAR sensors, synchronized via NTP, and evaluated against terrestrial laser scanner (TLS) or GICP reference models.

**Baselines:** KISS-Matcher inlier counts, root-mean-square error (RMSE) thresholds, and robust pose-graph optimization back ends including default Huber kernels and graduated non-convexity with a truncated least-squares loss (GNC-TLS).

**Reported findings:** The plausibility checks reject 37.3% of incorrect pairwise registrations while falsely rejecting only 0.6% of correct ones, and the full-team pipeline achieves successful alignment in 73.9% of evaluated snapshots with a mean final pose error of 5.40 cm and 0.51 degrees.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** The mutual-observation check primarily targets open, planar scenes and abstains in cluttered or vegetation-heavy environments where lines of sight are blocked, while the all-pairs pairwise registration stage scales quadratically and becomes a runtime bottleneck for larger teams.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The dataset, code, and implementation are publicly available via an open-source repository, tested on Ubuntu 24.04.4 LTS with an Intel Core Ultra 9 285H processor and 64 GB of RAM.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Investigating the combination of plausibility checks with other error scores like RMSE, extending the static-snapshot approach to mobile platforms to transition toward multi-robot SLAM, and parallelizing or accelerating the pairwise registration stage for larger robot swarms.
