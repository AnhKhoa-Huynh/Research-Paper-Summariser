## [PGX: Physically Grounded Execution for Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26596v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26596v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 9/9
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** How to execute long-horizon humanoid loco-manipulation tasks reliably using vision-language-action (VLA) models and classical control?

**Claimed contribution:** The paper proposes PGX (Physically Grounded Execution), a training-free execution framework that decouples task progression, temporal control, and spatial precision from VLA manipulation skill execution by using a predicate-gated finite state machine and a world-coordinate locking layer.

**Method:** PGX decomposes long-horizon loco-manipulation into three layers: (i) a predicate-based state machine that governs task progression via physical predicates; (ii) VLA skills that operate within active manipulation phases; and (iii) world-coordinate locking (WCL) that maintains spatial consistency through base station keeping, object-relative positioning, and world-anchored contact targets.

**Datasets/evaluation:** The evaluation uses four datasets of human demonstrations: 100 MimicGen demonstrations for box transport, 30 teleoperated demonstrations for mug placement, 50 Quest-teleoperated demonstrations for coffee fetching in simulation, and 44 Quest demonstrations for the real-world coffee task.

**Baselines:** Flat VLA models (which directly issue locomotion and manipulation commands) and FSM-only configurations (PGX without world-coordinate locking).

**Reported findings:** On coffee fetching with π0.5, PGX improves task success from 0% to 60% (nominal) and 55% (random init), while adding WCL-Pratives raises success to 70% and 70%. Across simulated tasks, PGX raises the average of stage progress and success from 35% to 78% for GR00T and from 17% to 82% for π0.5.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** PGX achieves higher success rates yet costs longer execution time for stage transitions.

**Reproducibility checklist:** The project page is available at https://kl-research.github.io/pgx, and simulation experiments are performed in Isaac Lab Arena.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work includes improving execution time for stage transitions and extending the framework to richer skills and tasks.

## [PhyDEX: A Benchmark for Physical Reasoning in Vision-Language-Action Models via Dynamic Extrinsic Dexterity](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26565v1)

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26565v1
**Status:** reviewed
**Pages:** 12/12
**Coverage:** full extractable PDF text

**Research question:** Can current vision-language-action (VLA) models accurately perform physical reasoning and dynamic manipulation tasks like throwing objects outside their kinematic workspace?

**Claimed contribution:** The paper introduces PhyDEX, a benchmark and scalable automated data-generation pipeline for physical reasoning in vision-language-action models via dynamic extrinsic dexterity, comprising 35K trajectories across nine throwing tasks and 100 scenes.

**Method:** The benchmark uses a fully decoupled simulation pipeline combining GraspGen-X for grasp generation, cuRobo for collision-free motion planning, and an SLSQP-based trajectory optimizer to generate throwing policies under randomized physical properties.

**Datasets/evaluation:** PhyDEX contains 35,357 episodes spanning nine tasks, 100 scenes, and 107 curated object assets categorized into uniform, irregular, and dynamic objects with static single, static multiple, and moving destinations.

**Baselines:** GR00T N1.7, DynamicVLA, and ForceVLA.

**Reported findings:** All evaluated VLA policies achieve overall success rates below 3% on PhyDEX, with pre-throw failures accounting for roughly 89% of all failed rollouts.

**Limitations/threats to validity:** The study focuses solely on a single dynamic skill (throwing) with simplified physical property randomizations (mass, center of mass, and friction) and evaluates only three representative VLA models.

**Reproducibility checklist:** Data generation is built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim across 8 GPUs, using 16 NVIDIA A100 GPUs for fine-tuning policies over 50 evaluation rollouts per task.

**Recommended follow-up experiments:** Future work includes extending the benchmark to more skills and richer physics like elasticity, exploring reinforcement learning and in-context learning, and developing explicit physical property estimation mechanisms.
