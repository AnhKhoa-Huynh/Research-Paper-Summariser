**arXiv** · 2026-11-13 · Score 8.6 · Human–robot interaction, Robotics and autonomy

The authors demonstrate that standard cooperative self-play handover policies fail when interacting with unfamiliar partners due to rigid timing expectations. To address this, they introduce hidden start-delay randomisation, which improves generalization to unseen partners without requiring partner retraining.

**Method:** Alternating PPO self-play in a physics-based parcel handover task between a Unitree G1 and a learned giver, paired interventions, and hidden start-delay randomisation (HSD).
**Claimed finding:** Standard self-play handovers drop significantly in success rate with unfamiliar partners, but adding hidden start-delay randomisation raises success with unseen scripted partners from 31% to 80% while reducing high-force contact.
