The authors introduce DiffPhD, a unified GPU-accelerated differentiable Projective Dynamics framework designed to simulate heterogeneous materials involving extreme stiffness contrasts, hyperelasticity, and contact-rich interactions. **Method:** The framework integrates stiffness-aware projective weights, trust-region eigenvalue filtering lifted to the backward pass, a type-II Anderson Acceleration scheme with dual-gate convergence, and a unified GPU pipeline that reuses a single sparse factor across forward, backward, and contact computations with stiffness-amplified Rayleigh damping.
**Claimed finding:** DiffPhD achieves analytic adjoints with machine-precision on the elastic path and bounded and quantified under contact, delivers up to an order-of-magnitude speedup over prior differentiable solvers, and remains convergent on stiffness contrasts up to 100 times.

The paper introduces CIALLO, an action codec designed to learn shared intent representations that bridge control interface differences across various robot embodiments. **Method:** CIALLO aligns actions, visual changes, and observation-grounded language in a shared intent space and adapts to new robots by updating only embodiment embeddings using short recordings. The authors instantiate a policy called Qwen-CIALLO by combining CIALLO with a Qwen3.5-4B backbone, and also evaluate finetuning the action decoder in simulation.
