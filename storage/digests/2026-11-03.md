The authors introduce ReContact to reconstruct and retarget contact-rich human-object interactions from video for humanoid manipulation. The approach uses an object-centric contact plan and a residual policy to stabilize tracking through occlusions and ensure proper contact forces.

### 2. [KlaskTron-IL: A Contact-Rich Adversarial Imitation Learning Benchmark with a Validated Sim-to-Real Bridge](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03091v1)
**arXiv** · 2026-11-02 · Score 14.3 · Human–robot interaction, Dexterous manipulation and embodied AI, Robotics and autonomy

The authors introduce KlaskTron-IL, an imitation learning benchmark featuring a multi-tier dataset and diagnostic baselines designed for contact-rich adversarial dynamics. They also demonstrate the zero-shot transfer of a trained behavioral cloning policy to the physical KlaskTron hardware platform.

**Method:** The authors collected 80k human demonstration timesteps directly on the physical robot, expanded them with 320k symmetry-augmented timesteps, and generated synthetic expert trajectories up to 5M steps using a PPO policy. They evaluated diagnostic baselines including behavioral cloning and generative adversarial imitation learning on the platform.
**Claimed finding:** Standard imitation learning methods exhibit three characteristic failure modes under contact-rich adversarial dynamics, specifically a data scaling plateau for behavioral cloning, a weak-gradient discriminator in GAIL, and an asymmetric behavioral cloning prior and reward-density interaction.
**Evidence gaps:** Commands used by the transferred policy must respect the actuator model's identification range of plus or minus 0.2 meters per second.
**Relevance:** Policies trained on this benchmark can run unchanged on the physical robot due to a validated sim-to-real actuator model bridge, supporting zero-shot hardware transfer for contact-rich adversarial reinforcement learning.
**Summary basis:** gemini from abstract

**arXiv** · 2026-11-02 · Score 14.1 · Human–robot interaction, Dexterous manipulation and embodied AI, Robotics and autonomy

The authors propose ForceLDM, a force-aware latent dynamics model designed to improve robot policy learning for contact-rich manipulation tasks. The approach uses knowledge distillation to train a student network on current observations to reason about upcoming physical dynamics, guided by a teacher network leveraging privileged future signals.

**Method:** A teacher network extracts future dynamic features using privileged future force/torque and optical flow signals, which are then distilled into a student network trained exclusively on current observations, combined with a curriculum-based progressive noise injection strategy.
**Claimed finding:** ForceLDM outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across five real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks while generalizing effectively to novel objects and remaining robust to environmental perturbations.
