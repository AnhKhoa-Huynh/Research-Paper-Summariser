### 2. [MOMASPLAT: Dynamic Novel-View Demonstration Augmentation for Mobile Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08372v1)
**arXiv** · 2026-11-05 · Score 10.45 · Dexterous manipulation and embodied AI, Robotics and autonomy

Momasplat is a novel-view demonstration augmentation framework designed for mobile manipulation to address policy sensitivity to camera viewpoint changes. The approach synthesizes full manipulation trajectories from unseen robot base poses by separately reconstructing the static scene and the manipulated object.

**Method:** Combines 3D Gaussian Splatting, feed-forward 3D foundation models, and kinematics-based action retargeting to reconstruct scenes and objects from standard multiview observations, render novel viewpoints, and generate synthetic demonstrations with transformed actions.
**Claimed finding:** The authors claim that Momasplat more than doubles the average success rate of the strongest baseline, maintains non-trivial success at large base displacements where baselines fail, and reduces the need for large-scale real-world data collection.
**Evidence gaps:** Not specified in source
**Relevance:** It improves robot robustness to unseen base poses during mobile manipulation tasks, particularly by addressing dynamic object interactions that previous static scene approaches fail to re-render.

### 3. [H-TRIP: Exact Many-to-Many Distance Index for Multi-Robot Exploration](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08139v1)
**arXiv** · 2026-11-05 · Score 10.2 · Swarm and multi-agent robotics, Robotics and autonomy

The authors introduce H-TRIP, an exact distance index designed for multi-robot exploration based on Kleene algebra over the min-plus semiring for growing occupancy grids. The approach uses a spatial hierarchy and rank-one edge insertions to efficiently compute many-to-many shortest paths.

**Method:** Partitioning the known map into a spatial hierarchy with fixed boundary ports, decomposing unknown-to-free revelations into rank-one min-plus edge insertions, propagating decreased port-to-port summaries up ancestor chains, and applying a shared hierarchical lift.
**Claimed finding:** Sequential insertions preserve exact shortest-path distances on the grid, and dense many-to-many queries are up to two orders of magnitude faster than sequential breadth-first searches.
**Evidence gaps:** Not specified in source
**Relevance:** It accelerates in-loop queries during multi-robot exploration runs on growing occupancy graphs compared to co-scheduled baseline searches.
