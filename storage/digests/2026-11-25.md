### 3. [Simulator-as-Proxy World Model for Robot Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32786v1)
**arXiv** · 2026-11-24 · Score 12.2 · Dexterous manipulation and embodied AI, Robotics and autonomy, AI and machine learning

The authors introduce SimPWM, a framework that combines a calibrated camera, a rule-based simulator, and a video model to represent and render robot manipulation scenes from a first frame. They test the assumption that video world models actually follow the simulated proxy states by evaluating perturbed actions and observing inconsistencies between the video output and the simulation.

**Method:** Given a calibrated camera, the system represents the robot using its 3D model and interacted objects as 3D points from the first frame alone, uses a rule-based simulator with no trained parameters to move them based on actions, and renders the result using a video model.
**Claimed finding:** The arm reliably follows the proxy across base video models, while simulated objects reduce missed pick-ups and their effectiveness in video generation depends heavily on the specific base video model used.
**Evidence gaps:** The simulator carries items like cloth and rope rigidly, and the video model sometimes ignores the simulated state or infers grasps purely from arm motion.
**Relevance:** It provides a way to test whether video world models actually condition their outputs on simulated proxies or simply ignore them, helping to evaluate the reliability of simulator-as-proxy approaches in robotic manipulation.
**Summary basis:** gemini from abstract

**Method:** DexWAC uses an image-editing backbone to predict visual latents, proprioception, reward, and continuation at the endpoint of each action chunk, thereby generating complete imagined transitions without intermediate video frames.
**Claimed finding:** DexWAC outperforms large-scale pretrained vision-language-action and world-action model baselines in simulation and achieves a 75 percent average success rate on two real-world tasks.

The authors investigate whether current robotic manipulation policies can successfully operate in scenes containing reflections by introducing a benchmark called RoboDojo-Mirror. They evaluate multiple policies and find that mirror conditions consistently reduce success rates due to spatial mislocalization and phantom targeting rather than just physical collisions.

**Method:** The authors introduce RoboDojo-Mirror, a benchmark built on RoboDojo that compares a mirror-free baseline with single-mirror, dual-mirror, and mirrored-tabletop conditions across five different policies, complemented by real-robot experiments with $\pi_{0.5}$ and FastWAM.
**Claimed finding:** Every mirror condition reduces aggregate success rates by 10.8 to 56.7 percentage points across policies, reaching 62.5 points on the mirrored tabletop, with 50 to 88 percent of failures occurring without recorded robot-panel contact.

**Relevance:** These findings reveal a shared vulnerability to reflective environments across diverse robot policies and motivate reflection-aware evaluation of embodied intelligence.
