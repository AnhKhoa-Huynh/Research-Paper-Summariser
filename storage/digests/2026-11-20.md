### 6. [UniVEP: Unifying Velocity and Endpoint Prediction for Robot Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26103v1)
**arXiv** · 2026-11-19 · Score 10.2 · Dexterous manipulation and embodied AI, Robotics and autonomy

The authors analyze the different challenges of recovering actions from noise versus polishing clean actions in flow-matching robot policies. They introduce UniVEP, a framework that jointly trains velocity and endpoint prediction targets with consistency supervision to switch prediction types based on noise levels.

**Method:** Systematic comparison of velocity prediction, endpoint prediction, and velocity-weighted endpoint prediction across policies, benchmarks, and inference budgets, followed by joint training with consistency supervision that switches from endpoint recovery to velocity refinement as noise decreases.
**Claimed finding:** Endpoint predictions remain more stable under high noise while velocity predictions offer more accurate corrections near clean actions, and UniVEP achieves high success rates with reduced inference steps on RoboTwin and LiLa-WAM.
**Evidence gaps:** Not specified in source
**Relevance:** It improves success rates and efficiency in simulated and real-world robot manipulation while cutting inference latency.

### 7. [TacInno: Teaching Vision-Language-Action Models the Contact Dynamics Beyond Vision and Action](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26405v1)
**arXiv** · 2026-11-19 · Score 8.8 · Human–robot interaction, Dexterous manipulation and embodied AI

The authors propose TacInno to address the issue of inconsistent gains when integrating tactile sensing into vision-language-action models. The approach isolates tactile changes unexplained by vision and actions, using this residual as a supervision target during training.

**Method:** A TacInno tokenizer compresses future tactile changes into a discrete code conditioned on visual observations and executed actions, while the TacInno-VLA policy learns to predict this code without adding inference-time modules.
**Claimed finding:** TacInno-VLA achieved average success rates of 87.5% across six simulation tasks and 70.5% across four real-world tasks, outperforming the strongest visuo-tactile baseline by 10.7 and 9.0 percentage points, respectively.
**Evidence gaps:** Not specified in source
**Relevance:** The results demonstrate the effectiveness of tactile innovation as a supervision signal for contact-rich manipulation.
