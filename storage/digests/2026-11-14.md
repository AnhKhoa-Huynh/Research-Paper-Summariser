### 6. [Autonomous Mobile Robots on a Computing Field](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16652v1)
**arXiv** · 2026-11-12 · Score 2.6 · Robotics and autonomy

The authors introduce CABOT, a computational model where autonomous mobile robots operate on an active cellular automaton grid rather than a passive plane. This approach shifts the computational burden from strengthening the individual robots to enhancing the environment.

**Method:** The authors define a model where weak, oblivious, and myopic robots interact with a grid whose cells evolve synchronously through finite states, and they validate additional rule sets using simulation.
**Claimed finding:** With a small number of field states, the proposed model enables weak robots to achieve tasks such as line formation, gathering from arbitrary configurations, grid and torus exploration, pattern filling, and maze navigation.
**Evidence gaps:** Certain tasks like gathering and line formation from arbitrary configurations cannot be solved field-silently with any number of states, and some rule sets are validated by simulation only.
**Relevance:** It establishes that strengthening the environment with a cellular automaton can compensate for extreme minimalism in robot capabilities, offering a new resource measure for multi-robot systems.

### 7. [MotionSkill: From Bipedal Motion Video to Control across Embodiments](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16346v1)
**arXiv** · 2026-11-12 · Score 2.6 · Robotics and autonomy

MotionSkill is a framework designed to extract timed walking references from monocular video and adapt them for various bipedal robots in simulation. The system aligns motion data, uses inverse kinematics to map the movement to different robot morphologies, and trains residual policies in MuJoCo to correct joint commands.

**Method:** The authors align root motion and body landmarks in a common frame from monocular video, solve inverse kinematics preserving limb directions and target segment lengths under joint limits and closure constraints, and train a separate residual policy for each target biped.
**Claimed finding:** From initial states held out during training, policies generated by MotionSkill successfully completed 98 percent of trials on the original video and 26 percent of trials when the upper body was occluded for one second.
**Evidence gaps:** Not specified in source
**Relevance:** Reusing demonstrated gaits from video can reduce the effort required to specify the same motion for multiple different bipedal robots.
**Summary basis:** gemini from abstract
