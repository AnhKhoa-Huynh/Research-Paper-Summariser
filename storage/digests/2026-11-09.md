### 2. [Grasp-TAGS: Task-Aware Grasp Selection via Critic Distillation for Prehensile Loco-Manipulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09864v1)
**arXiv** · 2026-11-06 · Score 8.8 · Dexterous manipulation and embodied AI, Robotics and autonomy, AI and machine learning

The authors propose Grasp-TAGS, a task-aware grasp selection framework designed to improve goal-conditioned large-object rearrangement for mobile manipulators. The approach trains a unified reinforcement learning policy across various initial grasp configurations and distills the critic into a lightweight utility network to rank candidate grasps prior to physical contact.

**Method:** Training a unified PPO-based reinforcement learning policy across multiple initial grasp configurations and distilling the learned critic into a lightweight grasp utility network that ranks candidate grasps using the candidate grasp configuration and goal information.
**Claimed finding:** Grasp-TAGS achieves an average success rate of 89.84 percent compared to 79.87 percent for the best-performing single-grasp policies, and reduces final orientation errors across all evaluated objects.
**Evidence gaps:** Not specified in source
**Relevance:** It provides a method to select grasps that account for downstream manipulation performance in loco-manipulation tasks, improving rearrangement success rates and reducing orientation errors compared to fixed or random grasp selection.

The authors examine agentic manipulation policies, where generalist artificial intelligence models command robotic manipulators directly without task-specific training or demonstrations. They introduce AMP1, a reference harness functioning across multiple robot configurations, which achieves high success rates on specific benchmarks and real-world trials.

**Method:** The authors systematically analyze agentic manipulation policies using AMP1, a reference harness that runs unchanged across single-arm, bimanual, and mobile manipulation, and test it zero-shot on benchmarks and real-world tasks with and without a persistent memory store.
**Claimed finding:** The authors claim that AMP1 achieves state-of-the-art results on the RoboLab-120 and CaP-Gym benchmarks zero-shot, outperforming leading vision-language-action and world-action models, and that adding a persistent memory store further reduces failure rates on bimanual manipulation tasks.

The authors introduce a benchmark called Seeing What Matters to evaluate task-conditioned actionable spatial belief in simulated tabletop manipulation. The study measures whether view-selection policies can acquire specific visual evidence required by a manipulation command rather than relying on general scene description.

**Method:** The benchmark provides 860 frozen evaluation cases across three domains using two protocols: closed-loop actionability and same-object functional out-of-distribution generalization. The authors also test a diagnostic reference pipeline and seven vision-language models on these cases.
**Claimed finding:** The reference pipeline achieves 98.8% closed-loop and 98.9% functional-OOD success at primary budget settings, whereas seven evaluated vision-language models only reach 26 to 43 percent.

**Relevance:** The results suggest that spatial belief for manipulation should be evaluated based on whether it acquires the evidence a command requires instead of scene-level completeness.
