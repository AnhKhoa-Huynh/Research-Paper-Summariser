## Summary / source snapshot

The authors introduce ManiLadder, a large-scale simulation benchmark featuring 112 robotic manipulation tasks categorized into four difficulty levels and eight types. The framework uses a standardized flow-matching imitation learning policy to calibrate difficulty based on success rates and demonstrates that the task difficulty ordering transfers to the real world.

## Method / approach

The authors developed 112 tasks across four difficulty levels and eight categories, utilizing a fixed data amount and a unified flow-matching imitation learning policy under identical training and evaluation protocols to calibrate difficulty using success rates.

## Reported contribution / result

The designed tasks preserve their difficulty ordering when evaluated across different random seeds, policies, agentic learning systems, and real-world experiments.
