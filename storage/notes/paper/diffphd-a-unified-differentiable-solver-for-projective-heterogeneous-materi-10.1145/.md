## Summary / source snapshot

The authors introduce DiffPhD, a unified GPU-accelerated differentiable Projective Dynamics framework designed to simulate heterogeneous materials involving extreme stiffness contrasts, hyperelasticity, and contact-rich interactions. The method integrates stiffness-aware projective weights, trust-region eigenvalue filtering, Anderson Acceleration, and a unified GPU pipeline to achieve stable and efficient simulations.

## Method / approach

The framework integrates stiffness-aware projective weights, trust-region eigenvalue filtering lifted to the backward pass, a type-II Anderson Acceleration scheme with dual-gate convergence, and a unified GPU pipeline that reuses a single sparse factor across forward, backward, and contact computations with stiffness-amplified Rayleigh damping.

## Reported contribution / result

DiffPhD achieves analytic adjoints with machine-precision on the elastic path and bounded and quantified under contact, delivers up to an order-of-magnitude speedup over prior differentiable solvers, and remains convergent on stiffness contrasts up to 100 times.
