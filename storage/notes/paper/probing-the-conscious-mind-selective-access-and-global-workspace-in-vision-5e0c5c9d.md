## Summary / source snapshot

The study introduces the Multimodal Concept Lens to investigate global workspace theory in vision-language models by mapping intermediate states to a shared semantic space. The authors claim this approach reveals selective concept accessibility, causal downstream participation, and shared functional patterns across different model families.

## Method / approach

The authors introduce the Multimodal Concept Lens, a multimodal Jacobian lens using vision encoder and language decoder lenses to map intermediate states to a shared semantic space defined by the decoder's output vocabulary, alongside counterfactual experiments and two J-space metrics.

## Reported contribution / result

Multimodal concept information is readable and selectively accessible as tasks require, accessed concepts participate in downstream computation and reasoning, reliable selective access develops later than concept accessibility, and shared patterns exist across VLM families and sizes.
