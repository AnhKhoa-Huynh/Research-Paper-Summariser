## Summary / source snapshot

This paper examines the use of egocentric human video to scale robot manipulation learning by overcoming differences in morphology, viewpoint, and action space. It organizes this learning into four distinct paradigms and reviews the associated benchmark datasets.

## Method / approach

The authors define Ego2Robot learning through four paradigms: explicit embodiment transfer, explicit action and task supervision, egocentric video representation pretraining, and latent action and dynamics modeling, and they review relevant benchmark datasets.

## Reported contribution / result

The four paradigms derive different elements from human video, including robot-format demonstrations, interpretable supervision, visual representations, or predictive models of actions and dynamics.

## Limitations / evidence gaps

Challenges include physical feasibility, agent coordination, in-context adaptation, controlled evaluation, and predictive control.

## Why it might matter

It establishes how egocentric human video can provide scalable experience for robot manipulation despite missing robot actions and differences in embodiment and viewpoint.
