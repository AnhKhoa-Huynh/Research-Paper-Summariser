## Summary / source snapshot

The authors introduce a framework for dynamic human-robot role allocation that allows users to take the initiative during collaboration. It combines large language model reasoning for explicit preferences with Shannon entropy-based monitoring of human hand-object interactions to manage robot tasks and correct execution anomalies.

## Method / approach

A cost-driven Behavior Tree architecture is used alongside large language model reasoning to extract preferences from natural language instructions, and online human activity is monitored using Shannon entropy-based analysis of hand-object interactions.

## Reported contribution / result

A pilot study and a multi-subject experiment with 14 participants demonstrated that the allocation strategy enables intuitive and engaging collaboration without increasing the perceived workload.
