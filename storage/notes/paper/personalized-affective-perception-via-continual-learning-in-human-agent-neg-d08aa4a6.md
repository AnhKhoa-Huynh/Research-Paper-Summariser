## Summary / source snapshot

The authors propose an affect-adaptive negotiation strategy integrating Continual Learning with Imagination for Facial Expression Recognition to personalize valence-arousal perception during live interactions. In a study with 66 participants, the continual learning enabled agent achieved higher individual utility and elicited greater participant concession rates compared to the baseline.

## Method / approach

A counterbalanced within-subject study involving 66 participants negotiating with a humanoid robot, utilizing an affect-adaptive negotiation strategy with an asynchronous architecture that updates the facial expression recognition model in the background and converts differential feedback into utility adjustments.

## Reported contribution / result

The continual learning enabled agent achieved significantly higher individual utility and elicited higher participant concession rates than the baseline, while also yielding higher mean arousal estimates and a broader output range.

## Limitations / evidence gaps

Participant utility and Nash distance did not change significantly between conditions.

## Why it might matter

The outcomes support the practical value of coupling continual affective personalization with differential feedback for real-time human-agent negotiation.
