## Summary / source snapshot

This study evaluated how data-driven personalized gait trajectories in a multi-planar exoskeleton affect user experience compared to standard patterns. Through a within-subject experiment with ten participants, the authors assessed comfort, naturalness, and overall perception.

## Method / approach

A within-subject experiment with ten young unimpaired participants comparing personalized gait trajectories against two standard database patterns, utilizing a stiff position-derivative controller.

## Reported contribution / result

The authors found no significant differences in user experience across the distinct pattern conditions, though later trials received significantly higher ratings for comfort and naturalness than the first trial.

## Limitations / evidence gaps

Not specified in source

## Why it might matter

The findings suggest that users may adapt to exoskeleton walking regardless of the specific gait pattern enforced, emphasizing the need to account for user adaptation and subjective feedback in controller design.
