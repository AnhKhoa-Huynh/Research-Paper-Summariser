## Summary / source snapshot

The authors introduce HALO, a human-aligned data collection system designed for whole-body mobile manipulation that integrates kinesthetic teaching with a low-resistance platform. Additionally, they propose a processing method to align demonstration data with deployment settings, enabling policies to achieve high success rates on complex tasks.

## Method / approach

Operators guide robot end-effectors via kinesthetic teaching and move a low-resistance platform while performing bimanual manipulation, followed by deployment-aligned demonstration processing that calibrates platform motion and masks demonstrator hands.

## Reported contribution / result

HALO enables up to 2.9 times faster demonstration collection compared to Mobile ALOHA and VR teleoperation while maintaining comparable or higher success rates, and achieves a 73.3 percent success rate on a six-stage long-horizon mobile manipulation task.

## Limitations / evidence gaps

The close human-robot coupling introduces discrepancies between the demonstration and deployment settings.

## Why it might matter

It provides a more natural and efficient way to collect whole-body mobile manipulation demonstrations, enabling the training of policies for precise and long-horizon tasks.
