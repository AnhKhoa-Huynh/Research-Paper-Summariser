## Summary / source snapshot

When a robot practices without human resets, every attempt still costs wall-clock time, actuator wear, and sometimes a person's attention, yet the schedulers that decide what to practice next treat attempts as free. We present an open, low-cost testbed for measuring these costs: a self-built SO-101 arm runs two demonstration-trained ACT policies that reset each other, with a vision-based success detector and a ledger that records the time, joint travel, and human intervention
