## Summary / source snapshot

The authors examine agentic manipulation policies, where generalist artificial intelligence models command robotic manipulators directly without task-specific training or demonstrations. They introduce AMP1, a reference harness functioning across multiple robot configurations, which achieves high success rates on specific benchmarks and real-world trials.

## Method / approach

The authors systematically analyze agentic manipulation policies using AMP1, a reference harness that runs unchanged across single-arm, bimanual, and mobile manipulation, and test it zero-shot on benchmarks and real-world tasks with and without a persistent memory store.

## Reported contribution / result

The authors claim that AMP1 achieves state-of-the-art results on the RoboLab-120 and CaP-Gym benchmarks zero-shot, outperforming leading vision-language-action and world-action models, and that adding a persistent memory store further reduces failure rates on bimanual manipulation tasks.
