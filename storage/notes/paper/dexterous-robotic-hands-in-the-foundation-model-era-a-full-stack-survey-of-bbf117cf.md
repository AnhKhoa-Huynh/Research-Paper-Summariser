## Summary / source snapshot

This paper presents a full-stack survey reviewing research on dexterous robotic hands from January 2024 to early November 2026. It organizes the literature across five interdependent layers, examining hardware, data, transfer, learning, and evaluation.

## Method / approach

A structured narrative synthesis of literature spanning hardware, data collection and curation, cross-embodiment transfer, learning and foundation models, and evaluation and applications, supported by comparative tables and case analyses.

## Reported contribution / result

The authors conclude that the utility of human data relies on alignment rather than volume, shared policies do not eliminate embodiment-specific constraints, and mere task completion fails to prove physical correctness or deployment readiness.

## Limitations / evidence gaps

Evidence remains constrained regarding the zero-shot transfer of contact-rich skills across morphologically distinct hands, long-duration reliability, and cross-sensor tactile generalization.

## Why it might matter

It connects design choices across multiple interdependent layers and proposes reporting interfaces and controlled comparisons to make full-stack progress in robotic dexterity more measurable.
