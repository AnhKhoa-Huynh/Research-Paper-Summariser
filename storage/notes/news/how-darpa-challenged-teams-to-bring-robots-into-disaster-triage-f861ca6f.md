## Summary / source snapshot

Ten engineering teams competed in a nine-day DARPA Triage Challenge at Disaster City in Texas to test how autonomous robots and drones can assist human medics in mass casualty events. The event featured elaborately staged scenarios, such as a tornado-ravaged market and a nighttime military base strike, designed to evaluate robot navigation and remote trauma assessment.

## Method / approach

Teams deployed custom-built autonomous ground robots and aerial drones using onboard cameras, lidar, and machine learning to locate mock victims, collect vitals, and assess trauma during multi-stage trials involving simulated emergencies.

## Limitations / evidence gaps

Wheeled robots struggled with stairs, entryways, and rough terrain littered with rubble, leading some teams to incorporate four-legged robots; student teams also faced hardware challenges like broken motors and parts upon arrival.

## Why it might matter

According to publisher sources, the technologies developed in the competition could help real-world decision-making in urgent mass casualty events, potentially saving lives and reducing uncertainty for medics operating with finite resources.
